📖 Station 3: Near Vision Card

Hold the screen at reading distance, 16 inches (40 cm), about the length of your forearm plus hand. 3rd class needs 20/40 up close. Readers allowed. Uses your calibration from the Eye Chart station.

Tap the smallest line you can read without squinting.

🌈 Station 4: Color Vision Plates

Pseudo-Ishihara dot plates, freshly baked with random numbers each time. Type the number you see. The AME checks this because runway lights and light-gun signals are red, green and white.

🎯 Station 5: Peripheral Vision

The AME wiggles fingers at the edge of your vision. We flash dots. Sit close (about 12 in / 30 cm), cover one eye or not, and stare at the yellow +. Hit space or tap the black area whenever you spot a flash. No peeking!

Eye doctors use this grid to spot trouble in your central vision. Hold it at reading distance, cover one eye, and look at the center dot. Do all lines look straight, with no missing or blurry patches? If something looks wavy or missing, that's worth a real eye exam.

👂 Station 6: Hearing Booth

Headphones work best. Start with your volume at about half. The AME's version is a whisper test: you face away and repeat what they say from 6 feet.

Pips start very quiet and get louder. Press the big button (or space) as soon as you hear them. We check each ear at 6 pitches.

🏋️ Station 8: Eye Gym (Bates method & friends)

🤓 Real talk from Dr. Kent: The Bates method is from 1920, and studies have not shown that it fixes nearsightedness, farsightedness or astigmatism. These exercises can feel relaxing and help with screen strain (the 20-20-20 rule is legit), but please keep your glasses for driving and flying. We skip Bates' "sunning", because staring at the sun damages your eyes.
